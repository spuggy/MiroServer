Your Capita-MiRo Team Report

This report has been built using the data from all the individual MiRo Behavioural Mode Assessments, belonging to the members of your team. It is intended to give you some indication of the expected behaviours and attitudes that might be at work within the group. In order to do this we have looked at a number of variables. Firstly the Leading and Supporting Modes at work within the team have been considered. The report will tell you where the balance of behaviours lies and will offer some interpretation of this result giving you some idea of the strengths and potential weak points within the team’s make up. Within those Behavioural Modes are also the perceptual and judgement biases present among this particular group of people. This report goes one step further than the individual reports by looking at the make up of the team as it relates to these factors too. This further enables us to look at the possible dynamic tensions within the team, which may be the cause of inertia and conflict or creativity and energy within the team.

How to use this report

As with any psychometric the MiRo Team Report can never be a substitute for direct knowledge of a team, which is why a qualified consultant practitioner always administers it. It can however augment that first hand understanding and the combination of the two can form a powerful resource, which can be deployed to help in many organisational undertakings such as:

The primary behavioural mode within this team is Organising, which will create a stable and thoughtful working group. This team is likely to be practical, caring and determined in outlook. Projects where human values are important will be likely to motivate them and although winning is likely to be seen as less important than doing a good job, most members of this team are sufficiently realistic about what is possible able to set plausible targets and work toward them effectively. This is a team of hands-on and values led people, which can lead to sound solutions to old and new problems alike but can also lead the team to avoid difficult or firm decisions. There may be a tendency to avoid conflict within the group and rapid change may be seen as upsetting or overly risky, this can be useful when consistency is called for but may need to be balanced against the occasional need for swift action. The team’s inclination to focus on the needs of individuals, while an asset, may also cause avoidance of or overreaction to situations in which people or accepted practices are seen to be in danger.

Although not the primary mode of behaviour at work here Analysing is also well represented. As a result this team can be practical, calm and detail oriented. They will work well when accuracy is paramount and although the desire to make things perfect may see tasks unfinished, the Analysers in this team are of a realistic nature and will be able to set targets and work toward them effectively. As practical and rational thinkers, they may ignore the human dimension of their decisions and while this can be useful when clarity is called for, it may need to be balanced against the need for effective relationship building. The team’s inclination to work systematically, while an asset, may sometimes also create a tendency to want to cover every available angle when the environment is uncertain and a more intuitive approach needs to be taken.

The Energising mode of behaviour is not in much evidence within this group. As a result the team is likely to be seen as dependable by others but may lack a certain creative flair. This can be a plus if the team is charged with tasks involving creating stability or dealing with crisis situations but where innovative and imaginative thinking or relationship building are called for, the team may experience some difficulties if it is not able to utilise the talents of it’s available Energisers or operate against it’s normal operational style.

In small measure but present within the team nonetheless is the Driving Mode. The group has access to all four modes and the Drivers bring their own skills and working style too. They can be focused and competitive, will relish working to targets and deadlines and their “can do” attitude will be a real asset. The Drivers may bring a degree of restlessness to the group and a desire to keep moving forward, but this is more than made up for by the team’s opposing desire to create stability. On the down side, the inclination among the Drivers to plain speaking and competitiveness may sometimes cause conflict within the team and with others outside of it.
