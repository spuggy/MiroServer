Your Capita-MiRo Team Report

This report has been built using the data from all the individual MiRo Behavioural Mode Assessments, belonging to the members of your team. It is intended to give you some indication of the expected behaviours and attitudes that might be at work within the group. In order to do this we have looked at a number of variables. Firstly the Leading and Supporting Modes at work within the team have been considered. The report will tell you where the balance of behaviours lies and will offer some interpretation of this result giving you some idea of the strengths and potential weak points within the team’s make up. Within those Behavioural Modes are also the perceptual and judgement biases present among this particular group of people. This report goes one step further than the individual reports by looking at the make up of the team as it relates to these factors too. This further enables us to look at the possible dynamic tensions within the team, which may be the cause of inertia and conflict or creativity and energy within the team.

How to use this report

As with any psychometric the MiRo Team Report can never be a substitute for direct knowledge of a team, which is why a qualified consultant practitioner always administers it. It can however augment that first hand understanding and the combination of the two can form a powerful resource, which can be deployed to help in many organisational undertakings such as:

This team contains only one active behavioural mode and the Energising mode of behaviour is not represented at all here. The team is likely to be quite limited as a result. This may not be a problem in it’s self but may leave the group lacking some of the advantages of teams where this mode is in evidence. The team is likely to be seen as dependable by others but may lack a certain creative flair. If the team is tasked with creating stability or dealing with crisis situations this can be a positive factor but where innovative and imaginative thinking or relationship building are called for the team may need to work hard to overcome it’s natural disinclination for this kind of activity.

The Driving mode of behaviour is not active within this group. As a result the team is likely to be seen as approachable by others but is likely to lack a certain competitive edge. This can be a plus where the stronger elements of the team are called upon to create a good impression or where constancy of approach is important but where tight deadlines are in play or difficult and high risk decisions need to be made the team may experience some difficulties if it is not able to operate against it’s normal operational style.

This team also tends to avoid using the Analysing Mode, which is not necessarily a problem but either as individuals or collectively the team may find it’s self having to work hard to emulate some of the positive attributes generally displayed by habitual Analysers. The team may lack attention to detail and an ability to stay calm in a crisis as a result. This can be a plus if the team is tasked with rapid innovation or needs to focus on the wellbeing of people but where an ability to see the flaws in a system or deal with problems dispassionately are necessary the lack of Analysing behaviour may be a problem.
